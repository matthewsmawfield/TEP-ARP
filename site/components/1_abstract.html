Abstract

The discordant quasar–galaxy pairs catalogued by Halton Arp — low-redshift galaxies apparently joined by luminous bridges, filaments, or foreground absorption to companions at redshifts one to two orders of magnitude larger — represent historical association claims that remain disputed and require independent distance adjudication. Under the standard interpretation, redshift is identified with distance through the Hubble relation and each such pair is treated as a chance superposition of unrelated objects. This paper applies the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) — the bi-metric scalar-tensor framework in which matter, clocks, and light propagate on the causal metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi$ with universal coupling $A(\phi) = e^{-\phi}$ — to a twelve-pair catalogue drawn from the published literature and verified against the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database. In TEP the observed redshift factorises into a background clock-rate factor set by the ambient temporal level hosting the source and an intrinsic conformal factor generated by the emitter's own proper-time field, $1 + z_{\rm obs} = A_0 / (A_{\rm bg}\,A_{\rm int})$. For two objects at a common distance the shared background term divides out exactly, yielding the distance-independent redshift-ratio diagnostic $A_{\rm pair} = (1 + z_G)/(1 + z_Q)$: a conditional estimate of the companion's local clock-rate offset only if co-distance is established. Applied to the catalogue, the diagnostic returns pair factors spanning $A_{\rm pair} = 0.33$ (NGC 7319) to $0.98$ (NGC 1232A); conditional on co-distance, these become a smooth distribution of local field depths rather than the discrete quantisation demanded by earlier intrinsic-redshift proposals (e.g., Karlsson 1971; Burbidge 2001) — while the standard reading places companions up to $\sim 360$ times farther away than the galaxies to which they appear connected. The physical-association evidence is evaluated independently of any redshift model: surface-brightness transects across the claimed bridges are tested against rotated control axes, the published Ca II and H I absorption systems fix the line-of-sight ordering, an empirical quasar-density test compares the Arp host fields against seeded random fields, and the Poisson chance-alignment probability is computed per pair and for the joint ensemble under selection-aware density and geometry conditioning. The resolved NGC 7603 filament is detected as real emitted light along its measured path ($+3.9\sigma$ over the inter-object segment, $+5.7\sigma$ peak, reproduced in three photometric bands). Demonstrating that foreground halo absorption fixes line-of-sight ordering without establishing co-distance, the Ly$\alpha$ forest path-length audit could be executed on one catalogue sightline (NGC 3067–3C 232) and falsified it, placing 3C 232 at its cosmological distance. The direct adjudication ledger therefore stands at one falsification in one key catalogue-pair test, with no pair yet confirmed co-distant and the other eleven isolated as unadjudicated candidates; existing HST coverage of Mrk 205 and Keck coverage of the NGC 7319 companion are non-discriminating. For the eleven-system residual catalogue, the joint probability is retained strictly as a conditional configuration-severity statistic, not as a co-distance measurement, returning $P = 3.6 \times 10^{-13}$ under the conservative empirical quasar density, sharpened to $4.9 \times 10^{-19}$ under the axis-conditioned geometry of the morphology-claimed systems and $4.0 \times 10^{-18}$ under the measured X-ray-selection density after correction for the measured pointed-coverage overdensity (yielding a look-elsewhere crossover volume of $\sim 310$ parent fields). Independent geometric-coherence tests — minor-axis anisotropy, radial redshift ordering, and physical-scale clustering at redshift-independent distances — probe structure the catalogue was not selected on; two return significant structure and the third is marginal, where the hypothesis predicts it, while paired-redshift and symmetry tests return bounding nulls. The scalar-field transition is inferred on the NGC 7603 filament — the only Arp structure carrying measured interior redshifts ($z = 0.243$ and $z = 0.391$ between the endpoints) — where the non-monotonic four-point transect refutes any single shared transition and is consistently parameterised by a nested-wells field model — compact local proper-time depressions superposed on the shared pair field — with each well confined to a projected width below $1.2$ kpc of its emitter and the diffuse filament continuum sitting at ambient depth between them. Transferring the historical Arp–Sulentic companion-redshift asymmetry test to an unselected survey-scale sample — $162{,}650$ bound satellites across $88{,}662$ groups in the SDSS DR10 friends-of-friends catalogue — detects a small, statistically significant positive redshift excess ($f_+ = 0.5075$, two-sided binomial $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-9}$, rising to $0.513$ in rich groups): the asymmetry strengthens in volume-limited subsamples ($f_+ = 0.510$–$0.513$), persists beyond host-disk dust scales with an inverted colour–magnitude signature, exceeds forward-modelled interloper injection by two orders of magnitude, survives at full amplitude in the clean spectroscopic-metadata and passive–passive same-template subsamples ($f_+ = 0.5078$ and $0.5092$), scales with satellite-to-central contrast ($\langle\Delta v\rangle = +5.9$ km/s at the bright end), and carries the opposite sign to standard cluster gravitational redshift — opposing the simple shared-ambient gravitational-redshift expectation and consistent with an emitter-intrinsic proper-time-well contribution, while residual magnitude-differential spectral-template systematics remain to be excluded. All tests are aggregated into a single falsification table separating pair-level distance adjudication from conditional configuration severity and population controls. A forensic re-reduction of the raw 2003 Keck/LRIS frames reproduces the published $z = 2.114$ spectrum of the NGC 7319 companion at its recorded wavelengths, and the same audit identifies the observation that would settle the question for the flagship system: a single blue-arm echelle exposure on the $z = 2.114$ companion of NGC 7319 would deliver the decisive $z > 2$ forest sightline. The eleven unadjudicated systems remain candidate naturally occurring differential-clock experiments selected for key follow-up; if co-distance is confirmed in any high-$z$ system, its redshift ratio becomes a direct measurement of the local clock factor predicted by TEP.
