The falsification table aggregates twenty explicit tests. 7.2 The Nested-Well Landscape

The non-monotonicity of the NGC 7603 transect deserves emphasis, because it is the point at which the data cease to be a catalogue of coincidences and become a measurement of structure. A shared pair field alone would produce a monotone; the knots' deeper intrinsic factors force each compact object to carry its own well. The filament is therefore not a smooth tunnel between two fixed ends but a landscape—a shared temporal field populated by discrete depressions wherever matter is compact. The bounded fitted width $w < 0.033$ (95% credibility, a projected $1.2$ kpc) localises each well to its object: these are proper-time features of the knots themselves, not of the ambient field. This is the scalar geometry of the TEP corpus made spatially concrete—the same universal coupling $A(\phi) = e^{-\phi}$, now read off as a map along an observed structure. No new mechanism has been introduced: the nested-wells family is the clock-rate hierarchy of Section 2 applied to a multi-emitter field, and it is the only family tested that can represent the transition at all.

A natural objection is that wells of depth $\Delta\phi \sim 1$, as measured for the NGC 7319 and NGC 3628 companions, should carry relativistic internal dynamics—velocity dispersions of order $c$ that would broaden every spectral line beyond recognition. The objection conflates a temporal well with a Newtonian potential well. In the dual-metric structure the scalar amplitude sets the local clock rate, and matter dynamics respond to the gradient of $\phi$ across the body, not to its absolute depth: a local laboratory cannot detect a uniform rescaling of its own clocks and rulers, and local Lorentz invariance is preserved exactly in every freely falling frame of $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}$. A well centred on the emitter is that emitter's own field—its gradient is the gravity-like Temporal Shear the object itself exerts, of order the object's ordinary self-gravity, not a foreign potential of depth unity applied from outside. The measured width bound $w < 0.033$ of the transect (a projected $1.2$ kpc) confirms the wells are object-scale: each depression is localised to its own source, and the observable consequence of the depth is precisely the endpoint clock ratio $A_{\rm int}$—the redshift itself.

Deep wells producing narrow lines are therefore not an anomaly for the framework but its defining feature: emitting matter sits at the bottom of its own temporal well, and the spectroscopic integrity of the lines is what a conformal clock-rate offset leaves untouched. The same reasoning disposes of the line-width form of the objection: the observed width $\Delta\lambda/\lambda \sim \Delta v_{\rm int}/c$ samples the field difference across the emitting region, $\sim |\nabla\phi|\,R_{\rm emitter}$, which is small because $R_{\rm emitter} \ll w$, while the redshift $z$ measures the endpoint depth $\phi_0$—the two are not in tension because they probe the field on different scales.

The distinction carries a history worth recording. When the Balmer-series identification in 3C 273 fixed the first quasar redshift at $z = 0.158$ (Schmidt 1963), gravitational redshift was the first alternative to recession examined—and was set aside because a surface potential deep enough to produce the shift demands a compactness no hydrostatic stellar configuration can sustain, an object driven beyond its Hayashi bound (Greenstein & Schmidt 1964). The objection is correct as far as it goes, and it does not reach the mechanism here. A temporal well is not a Newtonian potential and $A_{\rm int}$ is not the mark of an emitter sitting deep in foreign gravity: the offset is conformal, rescaling the emitter's own proper time while leaving its internal equilibrium untouched, so the compactness bound that closed the 1963 gravitational interpretation does not apply to it.

A related assumption deserves explicit correction, because the modern reader instinctively maps "compact quasar" onto the Kerr-metric supermassive black hole: an object carrying $10^8\ M_\odot$ of dynamical mass whose binding potential produces the redshift. That identification is not made here—but not because the companion is massless. A $10^8\ M_\odot$ object is a small fraction of the enclosed mass of the hosts that carry these companions and is dynamically unremarkable at that scale; nothing in the hosts' undisturbed rotation excludes it, and nothing in the interpretation requires excluding it. The identification is declined on a different ground: in this framework the redshift is generated by the conformal clock-rate offset, which is not a measure of binding mass at all. The deep wells of this catalogue are temporal wells in the sense of Paper 28 (Bahrain): as the local clock-rate offset grows, $A_{\rm int}$ approaches the temporal horizon $A_{\rm int} \to 0$ at which matter proper time asymptotes relative to ambient clocks. The deepest measured cases here ($\Delta\phi \sim 1.1$; NGC 7319's companion at $A_{\rm int} = 0.33$) are finite approaches toward that limit, not reached horizons, and the redshift they carry is generated by severe proper-time dilation in the matter metric rather than by immense Newtonian binding mass. The companion's intrinsic mass is left to standard accretion physics—reverberation-scale compact masses are neither required nor excluded; what is excluded is only the inference from redshift to gravitational binding. Well depth $\Delta\phi$ measures the conformal clock-rate ratio $-\ln A_{\rm int}$ and nothing further. This is the configuration that permits a discordant-redshift companion to sit embedded in a nearby galaxy—deep in clock-rate terms, ordinary in dynamical terms—without disturbing the host it accompanies.

A sharper version of the objection argues from the gradient itself: a well of depth $\Delta\phi = 0.3$ spanning the measured width bound $w \sim 1.2$ kpc exerts a scalar shear of order $c^2\Delta\phi/w \sim 10^{-3}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$, thousands of times the local galactic gravitational acceleration, which would shred the filament. The objection is correct about the gradient's magnitude, but misplaces it spatially. Confining the well to $w < 1.2$ kpc makes the wall gradient steeper, not shallower, but the four-point transect samples between the walls. The diffuse filament continuum between the knots returns $z = 0.030$, indistinguishable from the host nucleus: the field between the compact objects is flat at the ambient level. The kiloparsec-scale gradient is therefore confined to the unresolved compact object itself. What sits at the steep wall is not the ambient filament, but the object's own emitting mass.

The configuration is explicit in the exterior sector, provided two quantities that must not be conflated are kept distinct. In the weak-field static limit the scalar equation $\nabla^2\phi = V_{,\phi} + \rho_* A_{,\phi}$, integrated inward on the $g$-metric background of a compact engine with the unscreened isolated-body boundary condition $P = -2M$ (the interior-roll construction of Paper 0), returns the closed-form exterior profile $U(r) = -\ln(1 - 2M/r)$; numerical integration reproduces it to a fractional residual of $\sim 10^{-7}$, with the potential term negligible throughout the exterior. On that exterior profile the surfaces at which the field reaches the measured depths lie at $r = 5.8$, $3.9$, $2.5$ and $1.6$ Schwarzschild radii for $\Delta\phi = 0.19$, $0.30$, $0.50$ and $1.0$—for a $10^8\ M_\odot$ engine these are $\sim 4\times 10^{-5}\ {\rm pc}$, some eight orders of magnitude inside the $1.2$ kpc confinement bound—which is the sense in which the exterior tail is confined: the solution is nested on the ordinary galactic ambient $\phi_{\rm gal} = 2.85 \times 10^{-7}$, into which the unscreened tail merges at $\sim 30$ pc for such an engine, the same mechanism by which the galactic ambient level itself is set. The depth surfaces are properties of the profile, not candidate locations of the emitting gas, and the data enforce that reading rather than merely permit it. Bound line-emitting gas cannot exist inside $\sim 3\,R_S$, and at the $4$–$6\,R_S$ surfaces the circular-orbit speed is $\sim 0.3\,c$, where every line would smear to $\sim 10^5$ km/s. The re-reduced NGC 7319 companion spectrum returns ordinary broad-line kinematics—C IV FWHM $= 3{,}155$ km/s measured on the re-reduction itself, the detected UV complex spanning $\sim 10^3$–$7\times 10^3$ km/s—more than an order of magnitude narrower than any near-horizon placement produces. The measured clock factor is therefore the interior field of the object's own well: the emitting gas sits inside the deep region, where its motions sample the object's own binding—the well's self-gravity, not a foreign potential—and where the field across the emitting region is uniform at the level line coherence demands. That level is quantitative and discriminating: components emitted at field values spanning $\Delta u$ inside the well arrive smeared by $\sim\Delta u$ in fractional width, so the measured $\sim 3\times 10^3$ km/s widths bound the depth dispersion across the emitting region at $\Delta u \lesssim \sigma/c \sim 10^{-2}$. The required uniformity is quantitatively modest and is the regime the corpus's interior-roll analysis already imposes on the deep field: the rolling interior demands a locally flat drive on the potential floor (Paper 0, Appendix E, R3), so the deep region is smooth rather than structured—line coherence is a property of the well interior, not a tuning of it. Fractional line width is itself a conformal invariant: the endpoint clock ratio rescales every frequency by the same factor, so the measured widths are the intrinsic widths wherever inside the well the gas resides.

The wall shear at the $\Delta\phi = 1$ surface of the exterior profile, $\sim 3 \times 10^{5}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ for a $10^8\ M_\odot$ engine, is the well's own binding gradient acting on the emitting matter, of order the object's self-gravity at that radius amplified by the near-horizon $g^{-1}$ factor; it is not applied to the surrounding filament. Because the conformal sector preserves null cones, the deep clock well contributes no separate lensing convergence: lensing measures the $g$-metric mass alone, and a temporal well is lensing-dark beyond its actual mass. Two honest consequences accompany the result. The emitting knots cannot self-source such wells at gas densities—a 10-pc molecular knot's own field is $\sim 6 \times 10^{-9}$—so each well belongs to the knot's compact emitting core. And the unscreened exterior charge would double the Newtonian acceleration at kiloparsec radii unless suppressed by the nested-environment Temporal-Topology factor $S_\Sigma$, which is the corpus's stated screening channel; the solution is computed on the fixed $g$ background, with metric backreaction the regularizer of the near-horizon interior.

The bound $w < 0.033$ is an upper limit set by non-overlap of adjacent wells; the true scale of each well is set by its own emitting region, and the knots are unresolved point sources. Within each well, the shear is the object's own Temporal Shear—the gravity-like effect that binds it, not a foreign force applied to the surrounding gas. Across the ambient filament the measured flatness of $A$ is precisely the absence of inter-object shear. The depth of a temporal well is a statement about the clock rate of the matter inside it, and the transect shows the neighbouring matter running at the ambient rate.

A corpus-level consistency question follows directly. The decomposition $1+z = A_0/(A_{\rm bg}A_{\rm int})$ applies to every emitter, not only to catalogued companions, so if wells of depth $\Delta\phi \sim 0.3$–$1$ were generic to quasar-class objects, the redshift–distance structure of the distant quasar population would dissolve. The framework's answer is quantitative rather than rhetorical: the deep wells are a rare class, and the rarity is measured by this analysis's own controls rather than assumed. The redshift–distance map that quasar clustering and the Ly$\alpha$-forest BAO recover at percent-level fidelity at $z \approx 2$–$3$ bounds the population-mean intrinsic factor to at most a few percent, $|\Delta\phi| \lesssim {\rm few}\times10^{-2}$—two orders below the depths measured here—and the population tests bound the incidence directly: of 706 projected $z > 1.9$ quasars within $100''$ of 2MRS galaxies, none carries the path-shortened forest a local emitter must show (zero of 18 strongly discriminating sightlines, $f_{\rm empty} < 0.153$ at 95% confidence), and the forward cross-correlation returns no quasar excess around ordinary galaxies ($\delta = -0.030$). The catalogue does not sample the quasar population; it samples a discordance-selected tail of it, which is precisely what such a catalogue is constructed to isolate. Nor does the scalar sector need a mechanism that detects a neighbouring galaxy: under the association reading the correlation with the host runs through shared origin, not through the field — the companions are young emitters produced in the host's vicinity, and the knot transect itself measures the relaxation sequence ($\Delta\phi = 0.301$, $0.188$, $0.026$ moving outward along the filament) by which an emitter's own well declines toward the ambient level as the emitting region evolves. Well depth is then an evolutionary initial condition of the object, not a parameter fitted per system; proximity to the host is diagnostic of that stage, and the deep-well population is preferentially reached by any catalogue selected on discordant redshift. Under the projection reading there is no confinement to explain — the discordant fraction is rare by the same bounds. The consistent corpus position is therefore the one the data return: deep intrinsic wells are rare even among host-adjacent quasars, and any individual member's confirmation runs through the per-object adjudication ledger of Section 5.6 rather than through ensemble prevalence.

7.3 The Cost of the Standard Reading

The interpretation rejected here is not costless. To keep redshift as distance, the standard reading must place Mrk 205 at $\sim 300\ {\rm Mpc}$ while its host sits at $\sim 20\ {\rm Mpc}$, must read the NGC 7603 filament knots as background objects $\sim 0.9$–$1.5\ {\rm Gpc}$ behind a structure they visibly thread, and must attribute the ensemble's $\sim 10^{-13}$ joint probability to selection effects—an attribution the look-elsewhere computation of Section 5.4 bounds explicitly: a chance search reproduces all eleven residual severity classes with probability below $0.05$ only for parent volumes under $\sim 310$ fields, and no search volume produces the ordering evidence at all. It must also either place NGC 7603B $\sim 110\ {\rm Mpc}$ behind the host whose luminous filament it terminates, or—where the detected connection is granted as physical—assign the companion's $\sim 8,100\ {\rm km\,s}^{-1}$ redshift excess to a peculiar velocity an order of magnitude beyond any plausible filament motion; and it must turn the NGC 7319 quasar's $z = 2.114$ into a $\sim 10\ {\rm Gyr}$ photon journey from an object embedded in a nearby interacting galaxy.

Each of these is a distinct cost of the standard reading. What TEP provides is the alternative at fixed bookkeeping: a static spatial manifold, a single scalar field, and a redshift that decomposes into a shared background level and local depth—after which every anomaly above becomes a measured property of the local field rather than an ad hoc exception.

The standard cosmology's strongest independent support—the $(1+z)$ duration stretch of Type Ia supernova light curves—is also shared by the present framework rather than contradicted by it. In TEP the redshift is an endpoint clock-rate ratio; the same ratio that lowers a photon's frequency by $(1+z)$ stretches the observed duration of any source-frame process by the identical factor, since duration and frequency are reciprocal readings of the same clock comparison. Cosmological time dilation is therefore not an additional burden on the temporal-field reading but its direct prediction: a distant explosion observed $(1+z)$ times slower is the temporal field doing visibly what it does to every spectral line. The same axiom must then be applied to the companions without selectivity. Because the observed dilation is the endpoint clock ratio whatever produces the redshift, an intrinsic well predicts the same $(1+z_Q)$ stretch of a source-frame process as the cosmological reading does: a companion at $A_{\rm int} = 0.33$ should carry a threefold stretch of any genuinely clocked signature. The long-noted absence of $(1+z)$ dilation in quasar light curves (Hawkins 2010) is therefore double-edged and cannot be cited for the local reading—it presses equally on intrinsic wells, on the corpus's distant-quasar sector, and on the standard model itself, where the missing dilation is a documented anomaly rather than an explained result. Its correct role in this framework is an executable falsification channel, the temporal analogue of the forest census: a monitored companion that fails to show the $(1+z_Q)$-class stretch of a coherent signature falsifies its intrinsic factor without a spectrum. Nor is a clean single dilation signature expected a priori for quasars under any redshift model: variability generated across a range of depths within an emitter's own well superposes unequally dilated components, so the sharp supernova signature need not appear even where the clock ratio is real.

The same proper-time reading supersedes the periodic-redshift phenomenology long reported in this literature: preferred peaks near $z = 0.061, 0.30, 0.60, 0.96, 1.41$ (Karlsson 1971) motivated the discrete-quantisation proposals that dominated the intrinsic-redshift debate, but the verified catalogue returns a smooth continuum of $A_{\rm int}$ spanning $0.33$–$0.98$ with no preferred values. A continuous scalar field produces continuous depths; no quantised spectrum is required by, or indicated in, the measured distribution.

The same clock-ratio structure answers the surface-brightness objection, provided the bookkeeping is carried out on the matter metric rather than imported from expansion. The derivation is given in Section 7.4 and returns a sharper verdict than the objection anticipates: the Tolman scaling is recovered identically, $\Sigma_{\rm obs} = \Sigma_{\rm em}(1+z)^{-4}$, because the temporal-field redshift is a genuine endpoint clock ratio rather than a tired-light mechanism—the exponent is common ground between the two readings, and the distances entering the photometric relations, not the dimming, are the discriminator.

For the companions at issue the point is moot in a stronger sense: every quasar-class companion in the sample is an unresolved point source, so no extended-source surface brightness exists to dim. The expectation that a nearby quasar must reveal a luminous, resolved $L_*$ host presumes the companion is a mature galaxy at a local distance; in the framework under test the companion is itself the compact object—a young, high-shear emitter of the kind the ejection morphology implies—and any extended envelope it might carry is Tolman-dimmed by the full $(1+z)^{-4}$ regardless, so the faint, sub-arcsecond morphology the companions exhibit is the prediction, not the anomaly.

The same proximity that raises the apparent-brightness question lowers the luminosity budget it queries: at the host distance, with the transport correction of Section 7.4 included, the companions' inferred luminosities fall to the range of ordinary galactic nuclei, dissolving the energy paradox that made distant quasars implausible in the first place. In standard cosmology, placing quasars at extreme cosmological distances forces them to possess universe-breaking luminosities, prompting theories of super-Eddington accretion and billion-solar-mass black holes forming impossibly early. Because surface brightness is still Tolman-dimmed by the proper-time offset, a local quasar's true, un-dimmed luminosity falls perfectly into the sustainable range of a normal, young galactic nucleus.

7.4 The Photometric Sector: Tolman Dimming without Expansion

The objection answered qualitatively above admits a closed-form treatment, and carrying it out removes the objection rather than absorbing it. Photons propagate on the null geodesics of the matter metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi$. The disformal term contributes only along the field gradient: for the transverse, image-forming bundle—rays whose momentum is orthogonal to $\nabla\phi$ — its contribution to the null condition vanishes identically, and across the ambient field, whose flatness the NGC 7603 transect measures directly, its residual effect is second order in an already-small gradient. What the disformal sector governs is one-way transport time along $\nabla\phi$—the sector the closed-loop holonomy observable is constructed to detect—not the image-plane photometry at issue here. Conformal rescaling preserves null directions, so on the non-expanding gravitational manifold the spatial ray paths are those of $g_{\mu\nu}$ itself: static, and Euclidean to the accuracy relevant here. The photometric content of the framework therefore reduces to two ingredients—the endpoint clock ratio and photon conservation along straight rays.

The clock ratio enters the received flux twice. Each photon arrives with frequency reduced by $\nu_0/\nu_{\rm em} = A_{\rm em}/A_0 = (1+z)^{-1}$, with $z$ the total measured redshift—for the companions, the pair-differential component is carried by $A_{\rm int}$—and photons emitted over a source-proper interval $d\tau_{\rm em}$ arrive spread over $d\tau_0 = (1+z)\,d\tau_{\rm em}$, energy and duration being reciprocal readings of the same endpoint comparison. The photons distribute isotropically over the sphere of physical area $4\pi D^2$ centred on the source, where $D$ is the true static separation (the ambient convention $A_0 = 1$ makes the matter-frame and gravitational measures of the wavefront area coincide at the observer). The bolometric flux and the luminosity distance it defines are therefore

\begin{equation}F_{\rm bol} = \frac{L_{\rm em}}{4\pi D^2}\,(1+z)^{-2} , \qquad d_L \equiv \sqrt{\frac{L_{\rm em}}{4\pi F_{\rm bol}}} = D\,(1+z) ,\end{equation}

with the spectral form $F_\nu(\nu_0) = L_\nu(\nu_{\rm em})\,(1+z)^{-1}/(4\pi D^2)$ at $\nu_{\rm em} = (1+z)\nu_0$, so band-limited photometry inherits the standard K-correction unchanged. The angular scale carries the complementary factor. A source of transverse physical size $R$, measured on its own matter rulers, occupies a coordinate extent $R/A_{\rm em}$, because rulers inside the well are rescaled by the same conformal factor that sets the clock rate. On straight-ray propagation the observed angular size is then $\theta = R/(A_{\rm em}\,D)$, and the angular-diameter distance becomes

\begin{equation}d_A \equiv \frac{R}{\theta} = A_{\rm em}\,D = \frac{D}{1+z} , \qquad d_L = (1+z)^2\,d_A .\end{equation}

A temporal well thus magnifies: a deep companion subtends a larger angle than an ambient twin at the same separation—the conformal analogue of the $(1+z)$ apparent-radius inflation familiar from neutron-star gravitational redshift—and the two distance measures straddle the true separation symmetrically, $d_L\,d_A = D^2$. The Etherington reciprocity is satisfied identically, as photon conservation on null geodesics requires it to be. Surface brightness follows immediately. The observed solid angle is inflated by the well magnification, $\Omega \propto R^2(1+z)^2/D^2$, so

\begin{equation}\Sigma_{\rm obs} = \frac{F_{\rm bol}}{\Omega} = \Sigma_{\rm em}\,(1+z)^{-4} ,\end{equation}

the full Tolman law, recovered exactly—equivalently in one line from Liouville invariance: $I_\nu/\nu^3$ is conserved along $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$ null geodesics, giving $I_{\nu,0} = (1+z)^{-3} I_{\nu,\rm em}$, and the bolometric integral supplies the fourth power. The derivation exposes why the scaling could not have been otherwise: the relations above are formally the standard photometric system with the comoving distance replaced by the true static separation, because the temporal-field redshift is mathematically the same object—an endpoint conformal-factor ratio—that expansion encodes through the scale factor. The $(1+z)^{-4}$ exponent is a theorem of metric photon transport, not of expansion; a redshift that stretches arrival intervals exactly as it shifts photon energies obeys it automatically. What separates the two readings is not the dimming but the distance the relations are anchored to: $D$, measured independently of redshift at the host, against a $d_M(z)$ assigned by the redshift itself.

Applied to the companions, the photometry closes the debate at both ends. For the unresolved point sources—every quasar-class companion in the sample—the operative correction is the transport factor on total flux: an intrinsic luminosity inferred at the host distance is $L = 4\pi D^2 F\,(1+z)^2$, a multiplier of order unity to $\sim 10$ across the sample's well depths, and still two to five orders of magnitude below the luminosity the standard distance assigns.

For any extended envelope—the resolved $L_*$ host the objection expects—the surface brightness is Tolman-dimmed by the full $(1+z)^{-4}$: at $z = 2.114$ that is a factor of $\sim 94$, about five magnitudes per square arcsecond, pushing a nominal host envelope below the detection threshold of the available imaging; its non-detection is the prediction, not the anomaly. Should an envelope nonetheless be resolved, its angular scale reads off $d_A = D/(1+z)$ against the host's redshift-independent distance—a sharp discriminative test, since the standard reading places the same structure at $d_A = d_M(z)/(1+z)$, one to two orders of magnitude deeper. The magnification also tightens rather than loosens the compactness argument: the sub-arcsecond morphologies bound the emitters' physical sizes through $R < \theta_{\rm lim}\,D\,(1+z)^{-1}$, a stronger constraint than the Euclidean reading supplies.

7.5 The Historical Dimension

At the 1972 AAAS symposium (Field, Arp & Bahcall 1973), the dispute reduced to two background warrants—the defenders assumed the contested sky was a typical random sample, Arp assumed apparent proximity was physical—and neither side measured which held. The present pipeline is that missing measurement.

The present analysis validates specific observational elements of that programme: the NGC 7603 connecting filament is detected as emitted light, the silhouette ordering—the published photographic claim of Arp (1978), not reproduced on modern DESI imaging—records a foreground-background configuration, and the residual catalogue has quantitatively unusual angular and structural severity under the specified projection model. Individual co-distance nevertheless remains subject to the path-length ledger above. The proposed replacement for Arp's mechanism—intrinsic redshift as quantised, empirical, unexplained—with the deterministic proper-time gradient of the TEP framework: the discordance is what a scalar field produces, and the smooth distribution of $A_{\rm int}$ across the sample is the signature discrete-quantisation proposals (e.g., Karlsson 1971; Burbidge 2001) never explained.

7.6 Limitations and Open Questions

The limitations of the present analysis are stated plainly. The sample is twelve pairs—large enough to measure the chance statistic on the selected systems and to map one resolved filament, too small for population-level field statistics or an unconditional survey-wide rejection of chance projection. The nested-wells fit is a four-parameter description of a four-point transect: adequate at the error level, but underdetermined as a field inversion; it constrains the shape of the local proper-time landscape rather than the global coupling $\beta_A = -1$, which the analysis assumes. The model awaits over-constraint from either resolved spectroscopy of the filament at intermediate positions, or a second resolved filament with more knots.

The knot transect coordinates are archive-measured projections onto the resolved host–companion axis (the curved filament places the knots up to $23$ arcsec off the straight axis, which the projected coordinate absorbs), and the NGC 3628 companion resolves to WEE 51—the quasar at the tip of the minor-axis X-ray filament—whose literature redshift ($z = 2.15$; Arp et al.\ 2002) is superseded by the modern spectroscopic archives (NED $z = 1.4983$; Milliquas $z = 1.499$); the verification step adopts the archive value. The correction is verified to propagate only to the pair's redshift-derived entries (Table 1): the chance-alignment, member-ordering and physical-scale statistics consume the archive-measured separation, the member ordering and the host distance respectively—no companion-redshift input enters them—so the ensemble inference is identical under either reading. In the multi-quasar fields the archive cone does not always recover the published member list identically: the NGC 1097 resolution returns two of the four catalogued quasars at the measured offsets inside the enclosing radius, and the NGC 2639 members resolve to the Milliquas quasars nearest the catalogued positions ($z = 0.297$ and $0.336$ against the published $0.305$ and $0.323$). Both fields carry proximity statistics near unity under either member reading (Table 4), so the ensemble inference is insensitive to which set is priced; the resolved member table is nevertheless released for provenance. The companion $\sigma_z \sim 10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}$ uncertainties dominate the error budget where quoted, and SDSS spectroscopic coverage of the pair fields is partial. The ensemble statistic's dependence on the effective parent search volume is likewise bounded: the look-elsewhere computation of Section 5.4 shows the angular product decides the catalogue below $\sim 200$ fields and is an upper bound at any volume, because the observed systems occupy narrower classes than proximity alone—a qualitative severity bound, the conjunction itself not being a priced term in the model.

The most decisive open test is resolved kinematics of the filament gas: a velocity field across the NGC 7603 knots would separate a physically associated, possibly disturbed structure from a projected background filament. An exhaustive audit of the public spectroscopic archives (ESO MUSE, VIMOS, SINFONI and GIRAFFE; MAST JWST-IFU, MaNGA and fiber surveys) was performed at all twelve host positions and both knot coordinates, classifying every returned frame as science or calibration. At the NGC 7603 nucleus a MUSE science pointing exists at $0.9$ arcsec offset—IFU data over the host position already sits in the archive—but no frame is recorded at the knot coordinates, whose nearest science pointings lie $8$–$32$ arcmin away under the archive's proposal-target coordinates; footprint-level verification is required before coverage can be claimed. JWST NIRSpec/MRS coverage exists at NGC 7319, 4258 and 1097—the last also carrying MUSE, VIMOS and SINFONI science frames on the host—while NGC 3628 has IFU frames in the field but none on target. NGC 4319, 1199, 1232 and 3516 return no spatial spectroscopy at all; NGC 3067, 1073 and 2639 only unresolved fiber spectra. The decisive filament-kinematics measurement is therefore a new observation: a single MUSE pointing, or a NIRSpec-IFU mosaic, covering the resolved filament polyline and both knot coordinates.

The Lyman-$\alpha$ forest audit of Section 5.6 bounds the proximity claim from the opposite direction. On the one sightline where the test is executable the result is adverse to the association: the populated 3C 232 forest—nine Lyman-confirmed systems against $2.3$ expected—places that quasar at its cosmological distance, falsifying the NGC 3067 pair as an individual association while leaving the ensemble statistic intact. For the flagship high-redshift companions the test remains observationally unavailable: for the $z = 2.114$ NGC 7319 companion the raw 2003 Keck/LRIS-B frames have been re-reduced end-to-end from the archive: the frame headers name the target ``NGC 7319 ULX'', which is the companion itself (the ULX is its Chandra designation), and Galianni et al.\ (2005) record that it was placed at slit centre at PA $= 205^\circ$. The $9.4$ arcsec offset between the header pointing and the companion position is the telescope boresight offset, not a slit miss. The re-reduction reproduces the published blue-arm spectrum—the Ly$\alpha$+N V complex, Si IV–O IV], C IV, He II and O III]—at the published wavelengths within $\sim 13$ Å, after a $+44 \pm 4$ row flexure correction anchored on the slit-spanning NGC 7319 ISM emission ladder at $z_{\rm gas} = 0.022$. The red-arm C III] 5942 line falls on the dichroic throughput ramp and is not discretely recovered, and Mg II 8733 is outside the grating coverage. The same blue arm covers roughly two-thirds of the companion's forest band at $R \approx 1400$ and signal-to-noise $\approx 5$ per Å—enough to exclude a damped or Lyman-limit-class absorber, but not to census individual forest lines. The 2011 Stephan's Quintet slitmask allocated its seven extracted slitlets to low-redshift emission-line regions, none coincident with the companion position or carrying a Ly$\alpha$ emission line at its observed wavelength; and the nearest pointings to the NGC 3516 and NGC 1073 companions fall $94$–$101$ arcsec off-target, beyond the slit half-length. The $z \approx 2.1$–$2.3$ members of the NGC 3516, NGC 1097 and NGC 1073 fields return no usable forest-band spectra among the archive products inspected for each target. The audit thereby isolates the single executable observation that would settle the question for the flagship system (Section 8), and a blue-arm spectroscopic programme covering the four ground-accessible $z > 1.9$ companions has been drafted accordingly.

The archival pattern generalises into a selection statement about where the modern surveys can and cannot see these objects. The canonical discordant companions occupy precisely the angular regime that survey pipelines treat worst. In the imaging layer, companions projected within the luminous extent of a bright host are routinely shredded by, or folded into, the parent deblend and never acquire an independent photometric catalogue entry; in the spectroscopic layer, mechanical fibre-collision limits ($55''$ in SDSS-I/II, and reduced but non-zero in later surveys) exclude a companion fibre wherever the host nucleus has claimed one, so the closest and hence most discordant cases are the least likely to be measured. Downstream, friends-of-friends group catalogues impose a velocity window that rejects discordant-redshift members by construction—the sample the catalogue was built to characterise is cut out before statistics begin—and automated template fitting assigns low-signal spectra to the best-matching low-$z$ template rather than flagging a discordant candidate. Each stage can be audited against the public catalogues rather than asserted. Across the twelve fields, six contain no SDSS spectrum at all within $3'$ of the host nucleus, and none of the companions with resolvable coordinates has a spectrum within $30''$. The NGC 7319 case quantifies the mechanism end-to-end: the $z = 2.114$ companion is not a deblended photometric object at all—every catalogue child within $21''$ shares the host's deblend parent and is flagged non-clean, so the companion never acquired a photometric identity, was never targetable, and carries no SDSS spectrum; its only measured redshift comes from the single pointed Keck observation re-reduced in this work. The nearest JWST IFU coverage terminates $3.9''$ short of its position as well, measured on the actual cube polygons rather than pointing centres. The template stage is equally demonstrable: three spectra in these host fields carry a confident pipeline class and redshift at $\langle{\rm S/N}\rangle \approx 1$ with no warning flag, each resting on a single emission feature for which five to seven alternative single-line redshift identifications fit equally well. The scarcity of discordant companions in SDSS-era catalogues is therefore at least partly a measured selection boundary, not evidence of scarcity in the sky.

The NED redshift-independent distance library (NED-D) supplies the orthogonal discriminator that historically settled such cases. Companion coverage is sparse but instructive: NGC 1232A carries a Nearby Galaxies Catalogue distance estimate of $21$ Mpc—consistent with the NGC 1232 host distances ($\sim 15$–$21$ Mpc) and inconsistent with its own redshift distance ($\sim 94$ Mpc)—while the two photographic estimates for NGC 7603B ($214$–$232$ Mpc) agree with its redshift distance rather than the host's $\sim 115$ Mpc. The independent-distance record therefore divides: one companion sits at the host distance on a published (if coarse) measurement, one is weakly consistent with its cosmological distance, and the remainder carry no redshift-independent distance at all. Host-galaxy distances themselves are broadly consistent with their redshifts.

Extending the forest census to the full public spectroscopic sky returns a consistent picture. A cross-match of the DESI-DR1 forest-quality quasar catalogue (856,767 sightlines) against the same 2MRS parent sample yields 706 projected $z > 1.9$ quasar–galaxy rows within $100''$ of a bright galaxy, corresponding to 699 unique DESI spectra. Of these, 632 projection rows (625 unique spectra) have measured forest transmission and all show transmission within cosmic variance of the mean optical depth (maximum excess $+0.2\sigma$). The discriminating power is worth stating precisely: at $z \approx 2$–$3$ the cosmic forest is itself thin and a local emitter separates by only $\sim 2\sigma$, so the statistic only bites at $z \gtrsim 3.5$ where $\langle T\rangle < 0.5$. After rejected SDSS redshifts are removed, repeated DESI projections of the same spectrum are collapsed, and the single SDSS–DESI overlap is counted once, 18 unique sightlines occupy that strongly discriminating regime and none is anomalous. The exact zero-event binomial bound is $f_{\rm empty} < 0.153$ at one-sided 95% confidence. This is a bound on empty-forest projections, not a falsification of proximity for any curated pair, whose claimed connections were never tested by random-projection statistics. The elevated-transmission tail of the SDSS sample resolves entirely into template-stamped redshifts: 30 spectra whose pipeline redshift (up to $z \approx 6.6$) is rejected in favour of a multi-line alternative at $z \approx 1$–$2.9$ each show a normal forest once re-measured at the corrected redshift. The spectroscopic channel supplies no positive population candidate at its current depth; the catalogue-pair population that remains open is precisely the one the spectral sweep cannot reach. A photometric census of the twelve host fields finds 18 UV-excess point sources within $2'$ that carry no spectrum—nine of them around NGC 1073, the aligned-quasars field—while four hosts (NGC 4319, NGC 1097, NGC 1232, NGC 3516) return no catalogued point source at all within the same radius, masked by the bright-host deblend at even the photometric level. If a discordant companion with an unabsorbed blue continuum exists in this sample, it is in the unspectroscopied population, reachable only by pointed spectroscopy.

These results leave the measured structural observations intact—the NGC 1199 silhouette ordering (on the published photographic claim), the resolved bridge transects, and the conditional severity statistic on the eleven remaining systems—while fixing their logical status: they prioritize targets but do not establish co-distance. The filament transect is consistent with, and required to be, a nested-well transition; a population-level test awaits a larger discordant-pair catalogue.

7.7 Bypassing the Pipeline Deadlock

The diagnostic dilemma for testing the TEP spatial-proximity hypothesis is stark: broadband imaging destroys spectral dispersion at readout, while modern survey spectrographs destroy discordant targets in the targeting software. Because modern pipelines rely on robotic fiber positioners that suffer mechanical collisions, photometric deblenders that shred compact objects inside bright host envelopes, and clustering algorithms that reject velocity discrepancies by design, automated optical catalogs will not deliver the decisive high-resolution absorption spectra required to falsify or confirm co-distance.

Bypassing this deadlock requires exploiting observational modes that disperse light without slits or fibers, or datasets where full 3D spectral cubes already exist across the entire field of view:

  • The Macroscopic Continuum Drop ($D_A$ Flux Deficit): The cumulative effect of the Ly$\alpha$ forest creates a massive, measurable flux depression ($D_A$) in the continuum blueward of the quasar's Ly$\alpha$ emission peak. At $z \approx 2.1$, a quasar loses $\sim 15$–$20\%$ of its flux in this band. This flux step can be measured without echelle resolution using low-resolution spectra ($R \sim 500$) or calibrated narrow-band imaging surveys (like J-PAS).
  • Intervening Metal Lines (Mg II and C IV): While the Ly$\alpha$ forest lies in the extreme ultraviolet where atmospheric transmission is poor, heavier elements like the Mg II ($\lambda\lambda 2796, 2803$) and C IV ($\lambda\lambda 1548, 1550$) doublets appear alongside hydrogen and shift into the clean optical and red bands at moderate redshifts. Standard ground-based optical spectrographs (e.g., Keck red arm, SDSS) operate here with peak efficiency. A complete absence of intervening Mg II absorbers across a presumed $\Delta z = 2$ path length provides a severe probabilistic constraint on the distance.
  • Soft X-Ray Photoelectric Absorption ($N_{\rm H}$): Many discordant companions (e.g., in NGC 7319 and NGC 3628) are luminous X-ray point sources. Low-energy X-rays ($< 1$ keV) are highly susceptible to photoelectric absorption by neutral gas. A cosmological companion must shine through both the intergalactic medium and the host galaxy's entire interstellar medium, accumulating a massive total column density ($N_{\rm H}$). Because X-ray observatories like Chandra and XMM-Newton capture event files across open detectors without slit targeting, archival data can be directly fitted for the low-energy turnover to extract $N_{\rm H}$. A local companion on the near side of the galaxy disk will lack the expected absorption column.
  • Cold Gas Interferometry (ALMA / VLA): Optical telescopes struggle to separate a compact quasar from the blinding glare of its host galaxy, but radio and sub-millimeter interferometers isolate emission without starlight interference. Facilities like ALMA (observing CO) and the VLA (observing H I 21-cm) map cold molecular gas via 3D data cubes. If a radio/sub-mm emitting quasar sits behind a host galaxy, the galaxy's cold gas must imprint deep, narrow absorption notches against the quasar's continuum. Because interferometers record spectra for every spatial pixel in the beam simultaneously, the data avoids slit-targeting errors. An archival audit confirms 32 ALMA archive entries overlapping the NGC 7319 companion position (four programs, Bands 3 and 6), plus 4 entries on the NGC 7603 host and none at the NGC 7603B position; at the companion pixel the cubes contain host-velocity CO emission only.
  • Gamma-Ray Attenuation (EBL Opacity): The Extragalactic Background Light renders the universe opaque to gamma rays above $\sim 100$ GeV at $z > 0.5$ via pair-production annihilation ($\gamma\gamma \to e^+e^-$). A Cherenkov-band detection of any $z > 1.5$ companion would therefore be irreconcilable with a cosmological distance. (Fermi-LAT detections carry no such veto: LAT detects $z > 1.5$ blazars routinely below $\sim 10$ GeV.) A live cross-match of all companion positions and host fields against 4FGL-DR4 returns zero detections—including 3C 232—so no gamma-ray channel currently constrains the sample; the veto remains available to future CTA-class coverage.

Of these methods, extracting the X-ray column density from existing raw Chandra event files and checking existing ALMA datasets are the most immediately actionable, as they leverage archival data completely insulated from optical slit-targeting misses.

7.8 The Gradient Energy Density Constraint

The energetic bookkeeping that remains is real, but its operative geometry is not the pair separation. The measured field difference $\Delta\phi \approx 1.1$ is between the companion's well interior and the ambient pair field, and it is spanned at the well's own wall—not across the $9.3$-arcsecond ($\sim 4$ kpc) projected separation. The macroscopic-gradient geometry a naive estimate prices—a continuous field gradient filling the inter-object volume—is the configuration the transect itself measures to be absent: the filament continuum between the knots sits at ambient depth ($z = 0.030$) and the shared well width is bounded at $w < 0.033$ of the transect (a projected $1.2$ kpc). That geometry is therefore excluded by measurement, not merely by its own energy estimate. What remains to be priced is the microscopic wall enclosing each emitter's own well, whose only bounds come from the emitting gas's measured kinematics.

For a canonical static configuration carrying the displacement $\Delta u$ across a scale $R_w$, the gradient energy density $\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla u)^2 M_{\rm Pl}^2$ integrated over the configuration gives the enclosed mass equivalent $M_{\rm wall} \approx \kappa\,\Delta u^2 R_w / 12G$ in geometric units, with $\kappa = 1$ for a volume-filling ramp and $\kappa \lesssim 6$ for a flat interior bounded by a concentrated wall—the wall reaches the engine's own mass at $R_w/R_S = 6/(\kappa\Delta u^2)$. The emitting-region radius is bounded independently by kinematics: gas of intrinsic dispersion $\sigma$ remains gravitationally bound only inside $r/R_S \lesssim (c/\sigma)^2$, which for the flagship's measured C IV width of $3{,}155$ km/s is $\sim 9\times 10^{3}\,R_S$, and reverberation-scale broad-line-region radii are $10^2$–$10^3\,R_S$ for engines of the relevant masses. Two regimes separate cleanly. Under the volume-filling ($\kappa = 1$) bookkeeping that the corpus's graded interior-roll profile instantiates—the field rising continuously through the interior rather than through a concentrated wall—enclosures confined to $\lesssim 5$–$10\,R_S$ price at order unity against the engine mass for every emitter in the catalogue: a statically consistent deep-well regime exists at Schwarzschild scale, within which the measured $\sim 10^3$ km/s gas motions are comfortably bound ($v_{\rm esc} \gtrsim 0.3\,c$ at $10\,R_S$). A concentrated wall inflates the ledger by the profile factor $\kappa \lesssim 6$—for the deepest wells that pushes the unity radius inside the horizon, itself a selection for the graded interiors the construction supplies. At reverberation-scale radii the same fixed-background bookkeeping runs $\sim 10$–$10^2\times$ the engine mass for the deepest wells ($\sim 10^3\times$ at the concentrated-wall extreme). The quantity the wall contributes is itself part of the object's enclosed dynamical budget, so the operative exclusion is not whether the wall is massive but whether the total enclosed content exceeds the object's measured dynamical mass—a budget no existing observation fixes at these radii for these companions. The ledger therefore narrows the residual to a factor of $\sim 10$–$10^3$ at reverberation-scale enclosures under fixed-background accounting—the regime in which the corpus's coupled interior construction (Paper 28: a regular geometry with metric backreaction, demonstrated at stellar masses with the supermassive extension open) is the operative accounting—not the $\gtrsim 200\times$ parsec-scale exclusion a broad-line-sized wall would carry, and not the photon-sphere impossibility of the exterior-profile misidentification, which does not arise in this channel at all: the required enclosure is Schwarzschild scale or larger, the emitting gas sits at tens to hundreds of Schwarzschild radii inside the well rather than within $1.5\,R_S$ of a compact exterior, and the conformal sector preserves the photons' escape with only the endpoint clock ratio applied.

The Greenstein–Schmidt compactness objection is thereby met on its own terms. That objection assumed a one-metric world in which a large redshift factor is necessarily the gravitational lapse $\sqrt{1 - 2M/r}$, so that a deep redshift demands a surface potential no bound emitting gas survives. In the two-metric structure the clock factor is the product $A(\phi)\,N_g$: the scalar amplitude can be deep inside a region whose gravitational geometry remains regular and modest—the defining architecture of a temporal well—and the clock factor decouples from the lapse. What the objection correctly demands is a sourcing account, and this section supplies the quantified one: the enclosed-well energetics are order-unity at Schwarzschild scale for the graded interior the corpus's own construction supplies, the reverberation-scale bracket is a factor of $\sim 10$–$10^3$ depending on profile concentration under fixed-background bookkeeping, and closing that bracket is the same coupled supermassive interior construction the corpus already carries as open work in the temporal-well sector (Paper 28), here stated with its numeric margin rather than as an unquantified obstacle.
